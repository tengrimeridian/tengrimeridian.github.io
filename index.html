Tengri Meridian

Youth Cultural Diplomacy & Arts Initiative

A youth initiative from Kazakhstan bringing together students who write, draw, and work with traditional crafts — so our regions have a direct voice in cultural conversations with the world.

Why We Started
“Official meetings and protocols come later. First, people read an honest essay, look at a painting, or see hand-carved wood and begin to understand how another country thinks and feels. We wanted young people across Kazakhstan — both in major cities and in small rural settlements — to have a real place for that kind of conversation.”

The Idea

A Line That Connects, Never Divides.

A meridian does not split the map into a “center” and “outskirts.” It passes through different latitudes and ties them into one line.

That is the point of Tengri Meridian: connecting the cultural memory of the steppe with art and literature from around the world — from Astana and rural schools across Kazakhstan to Vienna, Berlin, Paris, Bishkek, and Beijing.

How We Work

We start with two things young people do best: writing thoughtful texts and creating visual or handcrafted art. Once our first two cultural events are behind us, we will open a third track for student research papers.

What We Do

Two Core Directions of Tengri Meridian

At launch, the initiative focuses on two tracks where school and university students can express their perspective directly.

Track I

The Written Word

Essays, cultural articles, reviews, and poetry. Clear thinking and honest writing without stiff bureaucratic templates — telling stories about history, language, and everyday life.

Essays Articles Poetry
Track II

Visual & Applied Arts

Painting, graphic art, digital illustration, and traditional craftsmanship (qolöner). Showing how centuries-old steppe patterns and handwork speak to a modern audience.

Painting & Graphics Illustration Qolöner Craft

Who We Are Glad to Work With

Tengri Meridian is open to cultural centers, teachers, and students who want real exchange rather than formal checkmarks.

  • 01 Cultural forums, institutes, and diplomatic missions interested in connecting with youth across Kazakhstan’s regions.
  • 02 Teachers, philologists, and artists willing to mentor students or share feedback on early projects.
  • 03 School and university students who write essays, articles, or poetry, draw, or practice traditional craftwork.
